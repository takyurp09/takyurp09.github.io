About

I am a PhD candidate in Environmental Economics at the University of Delaware. My research examines how climate change affects agriculture, food security, production, and economic outcomes.

I combine applied econometrics with climate-model output, satellite observations, process-based crop models, and large geospatial datasets. Across this work, I focus on transparent measurement, model validation, and policy-relevant interpretation.

Featured dissertation research

Combining empirical and process-based crop models for climate-impact estimation

My first dissertation chapter combines an empirical fixed-effects yield model with three process-based crop models for maize, rice, and wheat. The constrained ensemble is evaluated using leave-one-region-out validation and applied to yield projections under two SSP scenarios.

Show research approach

The project asks how observed yield sensitivity and the physiological structure of crop models can be combined without discarding either source of information. It emphasizes out-of-region validation and sensitivity across climate and crop-model inputs.
