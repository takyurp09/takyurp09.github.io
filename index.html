Research identity

Evidence across models, scales, and systems.

My research asks how environmental change moves through crops, firms, trade networks, and household welfare. I build empirical designs around climate and economic data, then use model comparison and spatial validation to understand where conclusions travel—and where they do not.

Alongside applied research, I develop reproducible computational resources in environmental economics, econometrics, machine learning, and resource economics.

Dissertation · Chapter 01

Combining empirical and process-based crop models for climate-impact estimation

How can the observed sensitivity of crop yields and the physiological structure of process-based models be combined without losing either source of information?

The project combines an empirical fixed-effects yield model with three process-based crop models for maize, rice, and wheat. A constrained ensemble is evaluated with leave-one-region-out validation and used to study yield projections under two SSP scenarios.
